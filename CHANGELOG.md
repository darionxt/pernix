# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the project will follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/) once released.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Sessions in the browser: a rendered Request under a Session is loaded in a browser context kept for that Session, which one fetch uses at a time; concurrent fetches of a Session get one each. A context is created with the Session's localStorage and the proxy of the Request or the Session, and replaced when either changes. Before each fetch it is made to hold the Session's cookies, with the Request's own for that fetch only, and after it, whether the page loaded or not, the cookies the page set, changed, or removed, and the localStorage of its origin, are kept in the Session, so a login in the browser carries over to HTTP and the other way round. Cookies keep their SameSite, expiry, host-only flag, Secure, HttpOnly, and path across the round trip, and the jar keeps what the browser does not tell, such as an unspecified SameSite and when a cookie was first set. At most `browser.max_idle_contexts` (8) contexts are kept idle between fetches, and one idle for `browser.context_idle` (300 s) is closed. Logins may render their pages.
- `CookieJar.discard(key)`, which removes a cookie and records the removal, as an expired one does.
- Logins and Accounts. `pernix.Account` holds login credentials: a `username`, a `password`, an `id` (the username unless given), `extra` credentials, and a `max_concurrency` of 1 by default; the password and the `extra` credentials are `Secret`s, masked in the Run's log. Accounts come from `sessions.accounts`, from `sessions.accounts_file`, a JSON Lines or CSV file (`pernix.account.read_accounts()`), and from `Spider.accounts()`, which yields both and is overridden to import Accounts from elsewhere, such as a database. With Accounts, the Session Pool holds one Session per Account, under its id, and no other. `Spider.login(session, account)` logs every Session of the pool in before its first fetch, those without an Account with `account=None`: one fetch at a time logs a Session in, in its place in its slot and under its Lease, which the login's Inline Fetches share, while the fetches that want the Session wait for it. `Spider.is_logged_out(response)`, a 401 by default, judges the Responses fetched under a Session; one logged out raises the new `LoginExpired`, a `Retry` with no delay, and the Session logs in again before its next fetch. A login that raises cools its Session down for `sessions.cooldown`, and after `sessions.login_attempts` (3) failures in a row the Session is Retired; a fetch logged out right after the login it made counts as a failure too. An Inline Fetch that shares the Lease of the fetch it is nested in, such as one a download Middleware makes, raises `LoginExpired` to it instead, so that it is the outer fetch that is retried once the Session logged in again. A stop cancels an import of Accounts that hangs. A Spider that defines `login()` or `accounts()` without a pool is warned about. Stats: `sessions.logins` and `sessions.login_failures`.
- `SessionPool.logged_in(lease)`, `SessionLease.must_log_in`, and the `login_failed` outcome: a pool whose Sessions log in gives a Session that must log in to one Lease at a time, until its holder logged in; `auth_expired` has the Session log in again, and `login_failed` cools it down and counts the failure, unless the Session logged in again since the Lease was given. `MemorySessionPool` takes `logs_in` and `login_attempts`.
- `RunLog.mask()` masks more values once a Run started, and `revealed_secrets()` finds the `Secret`s inside msgspec Structs.
- Session Pools, enabled with `sessions.enabled`: every Request leases a Session from the Run's in-process pool, `pernix.sessions.MemorySessionPool`, and is sent under it, keeping the cookies set during its fetches. Sessions are created as Requests need them, up to `sessions.size` (8), or all at the start with `sessions.eager`, and each takes `sessions.max_concurrency` (1) fetches at once. `Request(session="id")` leases that Session, created on first use, `session=response.session` continues one, a Request a Callback yields leases its own, and an Inline Fetch continues the Session of the fetch it is nested in or of the Request being handled. A 429 cools only its Session down, until its `Retry-After` or for `sessions.cooldown`, at most `sessions.cooldown_max`, while the domain keeps fetching with the others; a 503 still pauses the domain. A Request waits in the Frontier, without counting an attempt, while no Session is free, and fails with the new `SessionError` when none can ever be leased, or when it names a Session without a pool. Rendered Requests lease Sessions too. Stats under `sessions.*`.
- The `SessionPool` protocol (`pernix.sessions`): `acquire(session=, tags=, timeout=)`, `ready_in()`, `renew()`, `release(lease, outcome)`, `load_state()`, and `save_state(lease, delta)`, with `SessionLease`s that carry a token and expire after `sessions.lease_ttl` unless renewed, `Outcome`s (`ok`, `rate_limited`, `blocked`, `auth_expired`, `proxy_failed`, `error`), and `StateDelta`s that merge per cookie by modification time, removals included. A release or a save from an expired or released Lease is rejected.
- Sessions and the cookie jar: `pernix.Session`, with an `id`, its `cookies`, `local_storage` by origin, and an optional `proxy`, `profile`, and `account`, converted with `state()` and `from_state()` to and from `SessionState`, a msgspec Struct. `pernix.CookieJar` keeps full `pernix.Cookie` records, with expiry, Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite, and creation and modification times. `receive()` stores the cookies of `Set-Cookie` headers and `matching()` gives those a browser would send to a URL, by domain, host-only flag, path, Secure, and expiry, longer paths first. Cookies are refused, as Chrome does, for a `Domain` that is a public suffix (from `publicsuffixlist`, a new dependency) or another host, when Secure from an insecure origin or replacing a Secure cookie from one, for `SameSite=None` without Secure, and for broken `__Secure-` and `__Host-` prefixes; expiry is capped at 400 days. Neither cookie values nor storage appear in a `repr`.
- Fetchers take `session=`, the Session a Request is sent under. The HTTP Fetcher sends the Session's cookies that match each redirect hop with the Request's own, keeps the cookies set during the fetch in the Session as the headers of each hop arrive, even if a later hop fails, but not the Request's own, applies the Session's proxy and Profile unless the Request names its own, and gives each Session curl sessions of its own, so Sessions never share a connection. `Response.session` names the Session, and so do the Responses of `FakeFetcher`.
- The browser Fetcher (`pernix.fetchers.BrowserFetcher`), registered as `browser` by default and installed with the `browser` extra (patchright, pinned): `Request(render=True)` loads the page in Chrome for Testing, in the new headless mode, until its `load` event, within `browser.timeout`, and `browser.deadline` limits the whole fetch. patchright and the browser start with the first rendered Request; without the extra or the browser engine, it fails with a `ComponentError` saying how to install them. Each rendered Request gets a fresh context, holding only its cookies, as host-only cookies of its host in the order given; a Request other than a plain GET, or with headers, changes the first request of the page, and `Authorization` and `Proxy-Authorization` headers are refused, as the browser would send them on to other hosts. The body of an HTML or XHTML document is its rendered DOM, and that of any other the body received; `history`, `cookies`, and `ip_address` describe the navigation, and Chrome's network errors map onto the `FetchError`s. `browser.launch_options` are passed to patchright's `launch()`.
- The network guard covers the browser: a browser launched under it sends everything but loopback traffic to a proxy that refuses it, its own background requests included, records the requests its pages make to other hosts, and raises `NetworkBlocked` for a refused document.
- `pernix.testing`, a kit for testing Spiders without a network: `FakeFetcher`, answering Requests by URL or compiled pattern with bodies, Responses, exceptions, functions, or lists of them used in turn, and recording `requests` and the `unmatched` ones, which get 404; `make_response()`; `crawl()`, running a Spider to completion without waiting for periodic stats, retry delays, or per-domain pacing, and returning a `CrawlReport` of its Items, stats, reason, and `run_id`; `run_callback()`, running one Callback as a Run would; and `LoopbackServer`, serving routes, given up front or with `route()`, and the files of a directory on 127.0.0.1.
- A network guard: while `blocking_network()` is in effect, the HTTP Fetcher reaches only loopback hosts, or any host through a loopback proxy, checking each redirect before curl follows it, and raises the new `NetworkBlocked` otherwise. `NO_PROXY` in the environment no longer bypasses the proxy a Request names.
- A pytest plugin, loaded as `pernix`: the network guard is on while each test is set up, run, and torn down, fixtures of every scope included, failing the phase during which it refused a URL, unless the test is marked `pernix_network` or the ini option `pernix_network` is `allow`; and the `loopback_server` fixture.
- Inline Fetch: `await self.fetch(request_or_url)` in Callbacks, errbacks, `start()`, and `on_idle()`, and `Crawler.fetch()` for components, while the Run runs. The Request goes through the download Middleware and its domain's slot, waiting out a cooldown in place, or shares the place of the fetch it is nested in, such as when a download Middleware fetches a token from the same domain, still paced by the delay, the rate, and cooldowns; it is not deduplicated or stored, and no Callback runs for it. The retry policy retries it in place, emitting `RequestRetried`, and `RetriesExhausted` is raised once the retries run out. It continues the Session of the Request being handled, and is cancelled with it.
- Streamed Inline Fetches: `async with self.stream(request_or_url, max_bytes=...)`, and `Crawler.stream()`, give a `StreamedResponse` through the download Middleware, which see the Response marked with the new `Response.streamed`; the stream holds its domain's slot until it is closed, sharing it with the fetches made in its block. Fetchers stream with `stream()` (the `StreamingFetcher` protocol); others are read whole. Streamed bodies count toward `downloads.bytes` as they are read.
- A hardened HTTP Fetcher, configured by the `http.*` settings: a pinned `chrome150` Profile by default (`http.impersonate`), checked against curl-cffi's targets, with drifting aliases rejected; one curl session per Profile, kept in an LRU pool with idle eviction (`http.pool_size`, `http.pool_idle`); `http.timeout` for connecting, the headers, and each pause in the body, and `http.deadline` for the whole fetch; `http.max_redirects` (20); and `http.max_body_size` (100 MiB), raising the new `ResponseTooLarge`. HTTP/1.1 for cleartext URLs, HTTP/2 connection reuse, decoded bodies without `Content-Encoding` and `Content-Length`, and `ProxyError` for a 407 from a proxy.
- `HTTPFetcher.stream(request, max_bytes=...)`, a `StreamedResponse` whose body is read with `iter_bytes()` or written with `save(path)`. curl stops receiving while 1 MiB of the body waits to be read, so a slow reader holds a bounded part of it in memory, with or without a limit, and a paused transfer never times out.
- The `pernix` command, also `python -m pernix`: `run TARGET[:Name]` with `-a`, `-o`, and `--run-id`, where TARGET is a `.py` file or an import path; `fetch URL` with `--fetcher`, `--timeout`, `--json`, and `-o`; `shell [URL]`, an interactive console with top-level `await` and `fetch()`; and `check [TARGET]`, which builds every component without opening it. Every command takes `-s KEY=VALUE`.
- A command-line settings layer above `settings=`, parsed like environment variables: `load_settings(command_line=...)` and `Crawler.from_spider(command_line=...)`.
- `pernix.runner.run_program()`, which returns the exit status instead of ending the process, and `pernix.run_assembly.DownloadAssembly`, the download chain of a project's settings.
- Building a Run reports the problems of components that do not depend on one another together, as one `SettingsError` listing them when there are several. `Crawler.addons` holds the loaded Addons.
- Spider arguments declared by the keyword parameters of `__init__` and passed as keyword arguments of `run()`, `crawl()`, and `stream_items()`, or as `Crawler.from_spider(args=...)`. Strings are converted to the annotated types as environment variables are, including JSON for containers, dataclasses, and Structs; problems, and a `ValueError` from `__init__`, are reported together as `SpiderArgumentError`. `settings` and `output` cannot name an argument.
- `Spider.stream_items()` and `Crawler.stream_items()`: an `ItemStream` of the Items that pass every Pipeline, holding one at a time so that a slow consumer pauses the Callbacks; leaving it early force-stops the Run.
- `crawler.stop(mode)`: a Drain (`"drain"`) stops claiming and finishes the work in flight within `shutdown.grace` (20 s), then turns into a Force Stop; a Force Stop (`"force"`) cancels the Requests in flight, returning them to the Frontier without counting an attempt, discards the Items not processed yet as `items.discarded`, and limits each component's close to `shutdown.close_timeout` (5 s). Stopping covers the opening and the closing of the components too. `ComponentLifecycle.limit_closing()` limits closing while it runs.
- `run()` as a program: the first SIGINT or SIGTERM drains the Run and the second force-stops it; the process exits with 1 when the Run failed, 2 for a configuration error, and 130 when a signal stopped it (`pernix.runner.ExitCode`).
- `RunResult(run_id, reason, stats)`, returned by `run()` and `crawl()`.
- Items of any common type: dicts and other mappings, dataclasses, msgspec `Struct`s, pydantic v2 models, and attrs objects, recognized by `pernix.items.is_item()` without importing pydantic or attrs, and converted for export by `item_to_dict()`.
- Item Pipelines registered with `pipelines`: `process(item, ctx)` returns the Item or `None` to drop it, or raises `DropItem`; `accepts` takes types or a function; batch mode with `batch_size`, `batch_timeout`, and `process_batch(items, contexts)`; retries with `retry_times` and `retry_on` (or `Retry`) and full-jitter backoff, counted as `items.retried`. `ItemContext` gives the Request and Response of an Item. Pipeline errors emit `ItemError` and drop the Item without fetching the Request again. Each call is limited by `items.timeout` (60 s) or the Pipeline's `timeout`.
- Failure Pipelines (`failed_pipelines`) that receive each `FailedItem(item, pipeline, error)`, with the built-in `FailedItemsFile` writing them to `items.failed_output`; Items a failure Pipeline loses are counted as `items.failures_lost`.
- A bounded Item queue between Callbacks and Pipelines (`items.queue_size`) that suspends Callbacks when full, processed `items.concurrency` at a time; queued Items and pending batches are processed before `SpiderClosed`.
- Feeds in jsonl, csv, and json (a streamed array), configured with `output=` and `feeds = {path_template: {format, fields, accepts, encoding}}`. Path templates take `{name}`, `{run_id}`, and `{time}`. Files appear atomically when the Run ends, also when it fails.
- `full_jitter()` in `pernix.retry_policy`, shared by Request and Pipeline retries, and `consult()`, which asks a retry policy and keeps the outcome when the policy raises.
- `FeedError`, raised when an Item cannot be written to a Feed; it ends the Run.
- `Crawler.started_at`, the start of the Run that Feed paths use for `{time}`.

- Logging on the `pernix.*` loggers through a bounded, non-blocking queue and a writer thread per Run (`pernix.logs.RunLog`): records go to the application's root handlers when logging is configured, and otherwise to stderr as text on a terminal or JSON lines elsewhere (`log.output`, `log.format`, `log.level`). A full queue drops records and counts them (`log.queue_size`). Records carry `run_id`, `spider`, and `request_id` from contextvars, and URL credentials, cookies, tokens, other sensitive names (`log.redact`), and `Secret` settings are masked. The root logger is never modified.
- Stats: `crawler.stats` with `inc`, `set`, `max`, `get`, and `snapshot`, under stable keys for Requests, downloads by status and error type, Responses, Items, the Frontier, the Throttle, event loop lag, and log counts. A `StatsSnapshot` Event and an INFO line every `stats.interval`, a final snapshot and summary after `SpiderClosed`, and `StatsSink`s registered with `stats.sinks`. Components can be injected with `Stats`.
- The `run_id` setting identifies a Run; `crawler.run_id` is generated when it is not set.
- `Spider.crawler`, `Spider.stats`, and `Spider.logger` (`pernix.spiders.<name>`).

- Per-domain politeness (`pernix.throttle`): each host has a slot held only while fetching, limited by `domain.concurrency`, spaced by `domain.delay` with `domain.delay_jitter`, and optionally rate-limited with GCRA (`domain.rate`, `domain.burst`). Workers skip slots that must wait, so one domain never holds back the others. A 429 or 503 with `Retry-After` cools its whole domain down, up to `domain.cooldown_max`, and Requests claimed for a cooling domain go back to the Frontier without counting an attempt.
- `Spider.allowed_domains`, enforced at admission by the built-in `OffsiteFilter` Extension, enabled by default: other hosts are dropped as `RequestDropped(reason="offsite")`.
- Retries decided by a replaceable `RetryPolicy` after every attempt: the default retries statuses 408, 429, 500, 502, 503, 504, 522, and 524, `Timeout`, `ConnectError`, `ProxyError`, and `Retry(reason, delay=None)`, up to 3 times, waiting as `Retry-After` says (up to 300 s) or with full-jitter backoff (1 s base, 60 s cap). Retried Requests go back to the Frontier with a delay and emit `RequestRetried`. The `retry.*` settings configure it, including `retry.delay_scale` for tests.
- Failure semantics: a Request whose retries ran out (`RetriesExhausted`), whose fetch failed in a way the policy keeps, or whose Callback raised is kept as a Dead Request, emits `RequestFailed`, and runs its errback, `errback(request, error)`, which may yield Requests and Items. Callback exceptions are not retried, and outputs yielded before them are kept. A cancelled Run returns Requests in flight to the Frontier without counting an attempt.
- `FetchError` and its subclasses `Timeout`, `ConnectError`, `ProxyError`, `TLSError`, `TooManyRedirects`, and `DecodeError`, which the HTTP Fetcher raises instead of curl-cffi's exceptions, keeping libcurl's error code.
- The Frontier contract (ADR 0003): `add` deduplicating atomically, `claim` under token-carrying Leases with priorities, FIFO within a priority, and skippable slots, `renew`, `ack`, `nack` with delays, `dead`, `counts`, `wait`, and `close`, with every write conditional on the Lease. `MemoryFrontier` implements it, and `pernix.testing.frontier` ships the conformance suite for other backends.
- Fingerprints: method, WHATWG-normalized URL without fragment and with sorted query, body digest, and declared headers, never cookies. `fingerprint.headers`, `fingerprint.sort_query`, and `fingerprint.function` configure them; `request_fingerprint()` is public.
- The Engine claims Requests under Leases, renews them while it works, acknowledges them once the Callback's outputs are added, and keeps failed Requests as Dead Requests. `Spider.on_idle()` can add Requests when the Run is idle; the Run ends once it adds none. `frontier.lease_ttl` sets the Lease lifetime.
- Onion Middleware (ADR 0002): download `Middleware` (classes, or functions with `@middleware`) registered with `middlewares`, and spider `SpiderMiddleware` (async generators, or `@spider_middleware`) registered with `spider_middlewares`, built and checked once at startup and opened with the Run like Extensions. `NodeMiddleware` adapts `process_request` / `process_response` / `process_exception` hooks.
- Fetchers registered by name with the `fetchers` setting and selected per Request: `Request(fetcher=...)`, then `browser` for rendering, then the URL scheme a Fetcher declares in `schemes`. Settings gain `NamedComponents` registries, merged name by name.
- Control-flow exceptions `IgnoreRequest`, `Retry`, and `DropItem`; `IgnoreRequest` from download Middleware or a Fetcher emits the new `RequestIgnored` Event.
- The full `Response`: immutable with `replace()`; text decoding that follows browsers (explicit encoding, BOM, HTTP charset, `<meta>` or XML declaration, UTF-8, detection, then the `response.default_encoding` setting) with WHATWG label mapping, recorded in `encoding` and `encoding_source`; selectors chosen by media type with `re()`, `re_first()`, and `jmespath()`; cached `json()`; `urljoin()` honoring `<base href>`; and the `history`, `elapsed`, `ip_address`, `http_version`, `fetcher`, and `session` fields, filled in by the HTTP Fetcher where known.
- Relative URLs in Requests constructed by a Callback resolve against its Response, after redirects and honoring `<base href>`; elsewhere, such as in `start()`, or against a Response whose URL cannot serve as a base, a relative URL is an error. Whitespace, tabs, and newlines are removed from every URL.
- A Section can check its values in `__post_init__`; the problems are reported with the other settings problems.
- The full `Request` model: `json=` and `data=` bodies, making a Request without an explicit `method` a POST, `params=` query parameters added to the URL, `errback`, `kwargs` passed to the Callback, `priority`, `dont_filter`, `fingerprint`, `cookies`, per-Request `timeout` / `proxy` / `impersonate`, and component-private `ext`; plus `encode_request()` / `decode_request()` for persistent Frontiers, with Callbacks stored by method name and JSON-only user data. `Response.cookies` holds the cookies after a fetch, including those set on redirects, for `Request(url, cookies=response.cookies)`.
- Events: typed `Event` dataclasses dispatched as observe, gate, or transform; an `EventBus` with priorities, per-subscriber timeouts, isolation of failing subscribers, and `events.on()` for methods; core Run Events (`SpiderOpened`, `SpiderClosed`, `RequestScheduled`, `RequestDropped`, `RequestFailed`, `ResponseReceived`, `ItemScraped`, and more), emitted by the Engine and the Crawler.
- `Extension` components registered with the `extensions` setting, opened in order and closed in reverse, with close failures grouped in an `ExceptionGroup`.
- Addons enabled with the `addons` setting by entry point name or import path, with an `api_version` check, a settings layer, and a registration-only `setup()`.
- Layered settings: built-in defaults, Addons, `pernix.toml` / `[tool.pernix]`, `PERNIX_*` environment variables, `Spider.settings`, and run arguments, validated at startup with suggestions for misspelled keys and immutable afterwards. Typed `Section`s, `ComponentMap` registries, `Secret` values, and component construction with injection of the Crawler, the settings, and Sections.
- End-to-end HTTP crawl: `Spider`, immutable `Request`, `Response` with CSS/XPath selectors, `Headers`, an in-memory Frontier, a curl-cffi `HTTPFetcher`, a JSON-lines feed, and `Spider.run()` / `Spider.crawl()`.
- Project scaffolding: uv packaging, ruff, pyright, pytest, CI on Python 3.13 and 3.14, Apache-2.0 license.

### Changed

- `Secret` moved to `pernix.secret`; `pernix.settings` still exports it. `sessions.cooldown` also cools a Session down after a failed login.
- `FetcherDispatch` passes the Session a Request is sent under to its Fetcher as `session=`, inside `pernix.fetchers.fetcher_dispatch.sending_as()`; a Fetcher without that parameter refuses such a Request. `Throttle.observe()` takes the statuses that pause the slot.
- `parse_set_cookie()` also reads `Domain`, `Path`, `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, and `SameSite` into `SetCookie`. `is_loopback_host()` moved to `pernix.urls`; `pernix.fetchers.network_guard` still exports it.
- `SpiderClosed.reason` and the `run.reason` stat gain `drained` and `forced`.
- An error that ends a Run is logged before it is raised, with its traceback unless it is a configuration error.
- A string that is not valid JSON for a container setting is reported as `invalid JSON`.
- From Python 3.14 on, the annotations of component and Spider `__init__` methods may name types defined later or imported only for type checking.
- The per-Request processing of the Engine moved to `pernix.request_processor.RequestProcessor`.
- Building and wiring the components of a Run moved from the Crawler to `pernix.run_assembly.RunAssembly`.

- `output=` writes jsonl, csv, or json by the file extension, as a Feed; other extensions are rejected. `pernix.feeds.JsonLinesFeed` is replaced by `Feed`.
- `ItemScraped` is emitted once an Item passed every Pipeline and was written to the Feeds, apart from its Callback. In Feed output, sets become lists, `Decimal`s strings, and values JSON cannot represent, such as `bytes`, fail the Run with `FeedError`.
- The Engine takes `put_item(item, ctx)`, an awaitable handoff, instead of `item_sink`.

- `StatsSnapshot` gains `final`, true for the snapshot taken at the end of a Run.
- During a Run, the `pernix` logger does not propagate: its records reach the root handlers from the writer thread, with the message already formatted and the exception rendered in `exc_text` instead of `exc_info`.
- A Spider object can belong to one Crawler only.

- `Extension` moved to `pernix.extensions`, next to the built-in Extensions; `from pernix import Extension` is unchanged.
- `RequestFailed` is emitted once per Request that fails for good, not once per failed attempt; the Item Events have `response=None` for Items an errback yields.
- The `Fetcher` protocol is `async def fetch(request)` plus `schemes`; Fetchers are opened and closed like other components and no longer check `render` or `fetcher` themselves.
- Requests are independent: cookies no longer persist from one Request to another. Pass `cookies=` explicitly, or use a Session Pool once available (ADR 0011).
- `Spider.run()` and `Spider.crawl()` take `settings={"concurrency": ...}` instead of a `concurrency` argument.
- `Request` refuses cookies that a `Cookie` header cannot carry as given: an empty name, a name with `=`, `;`, or a control character, or a value with `;` or a control character. The error names the cookie, never its value.

### Fixed

- The HTTP Fetcher reaches the host a browser does for international host names, on every platform. curl converted them to ASCII itself, and on Linux reached other hosts for some, such as `strasse.de` for `STRAẞE.de`, where `Request(cookies=...)` then never went. A host that is not ASCII, or holds percent escapes, is now converted as WHATWG URLs convert hosts before curl sees the URL, and `Response.url` names it in ASCII; a host WHATWG URLs reject fails the fetch with a `FetchError`, as browsers refuse such a URL. The network guard reads hosts the same way, so `localhost` in full-width letters, or as `local%68ost`, is a loopback host. `pernix.urls.url_host()` and `with_ascii_host()` give the host and the URL as the Fetcher uses them.
- Log redaction masks the user and password of a proxy given without a scheme, as curl takes it: `alice:pa55@proxy:8080` becomes `***@proxy:8080`, in messages, exception texts, and stats alike. Without a scheme, only a user and a password joined by a colon before `@` and a host are masked, so e-mail addresses are kept, `mailto:` ones included; either may be empty, and whatever is joined to them is masked too, as `proxy=` in `proxy=alice:pa55@proxy`. Every rule looks at the text as given, and again with secret values starred out, and values that several rules find, overlapping or side by side, are masked as one, so masking one value never hides another; a header name or quoted name is never taken for a user.
- The HTTP Fetcher sends `Request(cookies=...)` to hosts without a dot, such as `localhost`, `intranet`, or `[::1]`, which never received them. They are held for the Request's host only, for every path, so they go to each redirect hop on that host, whatever its port or scheme, and to no other host. Their order no longer depends on their names alphabetically: curl sends longer names first, and names of one length in the order given, in one `Cookie` header over HTTP/1.1 and a `cookie` field each over HTTP/2. Cookies named with the `__Secure-` or `__Host-` prefix, which were never sent, go over HTTPS, and to loopback hosts.
